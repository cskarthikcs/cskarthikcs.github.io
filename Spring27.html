Grading


Your grade is computed out of 1000 points.

Midterm I is assigned 200 points, Midterm II is assigned 300 points, and the final exam is assigned 400 points.

The remaining 100 points will be based on an oral examination, for which students may choose either material from the homework assignments or a bonus topic of their choice.

Your grade will be computed solely based on the points acquired through Midterm I, Midterm II, and the final exam. There are no homework assignments or extra-credit activities for the course. No exceptions.

Your total points accumulated is Midterm I + Midterm II + Final Exam, after applying the scaling policy below separately to each exam.

There is no curve applied to the final score: you must earn a minimum of 90% of the total points for an A+ (900 points), 80% for an A (800 points), 70% for a B+ (700 points), 60% for a B (600 points), 50% for a C (500 points), and 40% for a D (400 points). You will fail the course if you earn less than 40% (400 points).

The cutoffs are strictly followed for each letter grade. A computed grade of 79.99% is a B+, not an A. Scores will not be rounded up to meet grade boundaries. No exceptions.

For all three exams, the following scaling policy will be applied separately. First, each student's raw score will be expressed as a percentage out of 100. Let x denote your original percentage score, and let μ and σ denote the mean and standard deviation, respectively, of the percentage scores across all students taking that exam. Your adjusted percentage score will be

min{100, max{x, 75 + 12(x − μ)/σ}}.

If the standard deviation is zero, the original scores will be retained. The adjusted percentage will be converted back into points using that exam's maximum score. Thus, your score will never decrease because of scaling, and the adjusted score cannot exceed the maximum points available on the exam.

For example, if the mean score on an exam is 60%, the standard deviation is 15 percentage points, and you obtained 50%, then your adjusted score will be 75 + 12(50 − 60)/15 = 67%. If your original score is higher than the normalized score, the original score will be retained. Scaling is intended to compensate for difficult exams, and no further normalization will be performed on the final total.
